# Set Two Rules Supplement
*Every rule and term that exists only in Set Two. It adds to the full Crystal Quest Rules; nothing here is defined in the main rules, so players without Set Two access never see it.*

*Playtest · updated Nov 15, 2026*

## About This Supplement

- **Who sees it:** only accounts with Set Two access on Deck Forge.
- **How it combines:** use the main rules for everything not covered here. If this supplement and the main rules disagree, this supplement wins in games that include Set Two cards.
- **Status:** playtest only. Every rule and card here can change between updates.

## Set Two Rule Changes

These changes apply only in games that include Set Two cards. They are queued and not yet in the main rules.

1. **Poison is Health Point loss.** A Poison counter makes its target lose Health Points instead of dealing damage. It does not trigger effects that care about damage, such as Descale.
2. **Descale needs damage dealt.** Descale triggers whenever damage is dealt to the Hero, even if its Defense prevents all of that damage.
3. **Revenants and Health Point loss.** Impervious stops damage and destroy effects, but a Revenant still dies when it loses all its Health Points, including from Poison.

## Necromancer

Necromancers raise an army of Revenants, then choose between growing it and cashing it in for Aura, cards or bigger summons. They also use their discard pile as a resource and Mill cards to fuel it.

- **Heroes:** 0 Strength, 1 Defense, 1 Aura, 15 Health Points.
- **Revenants:** every Revenant is a 1/1 with Impervious. Summoning one always exhausts the Hero; each card says so.
- **Keywords:** Mourn, Exhume, Impervious, Mill.
- **Gaining Exhume** (Undead's Choice): "Target card in your discard pile gains Exhume this turn" means you may play that card from your discard pile this turn, paying its costs, even if it wasn't milled.

## Dragonborn

Dragonborn Heroes start with high Defense that wears down as they are hit. Opponents slug through their scales.

- **Keyword:** Descale.
- **Status:** in progress; Heroes and cards are still being designed.

## Artificer

Artificers make things. They play two-sided Recipe cards, spend ingredients from the party's inventory, and flip the Recipe to reveal what they made: Constructs, items or weapons for the party.

- **Heroes:** low Strength, 1 Defense, 2 Aura, 15 Health Points.
- **Recipe:** a two-sided card type. The front lists its ingredients and Aura cost. The back shows what it makes.
- **Craft:** play this only if you control its ingredients. Pay its Aura cost, sacrifice the ingredients, then flip the card. A made item goes into your inventory. Made equipment enters play equipped to the Hero that crafted it (if that Hero can't use it, it goes into your inventory). A made summon enters play, and the Recipe then goes to your discard pile.
- **Crafting doesn't exhaust the Hero.** A Recipe exhausts the Hero that plays it only if its card says "Exhaust this Hero." (no current Recipe does).
- **Aura cost of the made side:** the back of every Recipe shows an Aura cost equal to the total Aura cost of its ingredients + 1. For an "any" ingredient, the cheapest card that qualifies is used. For made equipment, this is the Aura cost to equip it. A Recipe that makes a Hero (Remey) has no printed cost on its back. Exception: Dragonscale Scale costs 6 Aura (set by hand).
- **Ingredients** can be anywhere in play under your control: in your inventory or equipped to one of your Heroes. Equipment used as an ingredient is unequipped and sacrificed. "Any Metal" means any Ingot. "Any Sword" means any equipment with the Sword subtype. "Any Shield" means any equipment with the Shield subtype. "Any Chest armor" means any equipment card in the Chest region.
- **Ingots and other Materials:** the Copper, Iron, Silver, Platinum and Mithril Ingots, the Pearls (Flame, Frost, Time and Aura) and Dragon Scale are generic items with the Material subtype, like the Set 1 Bars and gems. Each has its own use, so any class can play them; Artificers also use them as ingredients.
- **Tool:** the subtype of Artificer equipment (Tinker's Goggles, Bellows, Scavenger's Tongs, Wellness Rod) and of the Toolbelt, which is Generic: any class can equip it. Necromancer equipment uses item subtypes: Wand, Dagger, Amulet.
- **Quicksilver:** a special Artificer Material (2 Aura item). It does nothing on its own, but it can be used as any Material a Recipe needs (a Bar, Ingot, Pearl, gem or Dragon Scale, or "any Metal"). It can't stand in for other ingredients such as a Potion or a Sword.
- **Stall counters:** stall counters always go on a card. While a card has stall counters, it is delayed: it can't be played (or, for a card set aside from play, it can't come back into play). Each time a player in the party that owns the card takes an action, remove one stall counter from it. Stall counters stay on the card from turn to turn until they're removed. When the last one is removed, the delay ends.
- **Stall** (Pocket Watch, Time Pearl): the card being played is set aside with 2 stall counters on it. The Aura already paid for it is not refunded. When the last counter is removed, the card is played right away with the same targets (and choices), without paying its Aura cost again and without a new chance to respond. If any of its targets, or the Hero that played it, isn't legal any more, the card does nothing and goes to its owner's discard pile. If the card is equipment, it goes to its owner's inventory instead of being equipped.
- **Extra inventory slots** (Toolbelt): your party has one more inventory slot for each Toolbelt equipped to a Hero that isn't dead (two more if that Toolbelt is upgraded). If a Toolbelt leaves, use the normal inventory overflow rule: you choose which cards to discard until everything fits.
- **Made equipment** enters play equipped to the Hero that crafted it, without paying its Aura cost. If it later goes to your inventory, a Hero equips it again by the normal rules, paying its Aura cost.
- **Piercing** (The Archfrost, The Archflame): attacks from the equipped Hero ignore all of the target's Defense.
- **Dragonscale Scale:** the equipped Hero has Impervious (see Necromancer). It still dies if it loses all its Health Points.
- **Crafted:** everything a Recipe makes that is an item or a piece of equipment has the subtype Crafted (on its back, for example "Item - Crafted" or "Equipment - Crafted Shield"). Summons and Hero Constructs are not Crafted items. When a Crafted item leaves play, its Recipe card goes to the discard pile. A "Crafted item in your discard pile" (Crafter's Choice) is a Recipe that was already crafted and flipped and went to the discard pile that way. A Recipe that was discarded without being crafted doesn't count.
- **"Any Shield"** as an ingredient means any equipment with the Shield subtype or Shield in its name (Tower Shield, Shield of Riverstead).
- **Surrender a soul as a cost** (Remey Recipe): "As an additional cost to play this card, surrender a soul" means you choose one of your dead Heroes and surrender its soul (the normal Surrender Your Soul rules, without choosing one of its effects). You can't play the card if you have no dead Hero whose soul can be surrendered. It counts as surrendering a soul for Mourn.
- **Hero Construct** (Remey the Incredible, made by the Remey Recipe): a Construct that joins your party as an extra Hero. It goes at the end of your party order and acts Last (after the Closer). It enters exhausted. It adds its Aura at Dawn, earns Gold like your other Heroes, and can level and equip like any Hero. Its Hero type is Omni: it counts as every Hero type, so it can play any class's cards and use any class's equipment, and cards that care about a Hero type count it. It counts as a Construct for card text (Gadget Commander counts it). Your party isn't defeated while it's alive. When it dies it is destroyed instead of dying face down: it can't be revived, its soul can't be surrendered, and the Recipe card goes to your discard pile.
- **Deflecting Shield:** when an opponent declares an attack, card or ability that has a target, you may reveal the Shield like a Hidden card (no action needed) and redirect it to another legal target. Then exile the Shield with 4 stall counters on it. When the last counter is removed (your party has taken 4 actions), it returns to your inventory, and a Hero can equip it again by the normal rules.
- **Time Pearl:** when an opponent plays a card (equipment included) or equips equipment from their inventory, you may sacrifice a Time Pearl (no action needed) to put 2 stall counters on that card (see Stall). Time Pearl can't be used on a card already in play.
- **Crafter's Choice** "next Recipe this turn costs 1 less Aura" lasts until the end of the turn or until you play a Recipe.
- **Construct Recipes** (Frost Kat, Time Kat, Flame Kat, Z): the back is a summon with its own Strength, Defense and Health Points. It enters play exhausted, and the Recipe goes to your discard pile. Constructs with Defense reduce damage like a Hero's Defense. Z is a Dragon Construct, which counts as a Construct. Time Kat puts two stall counters on a Stalled card (any party's); if more than one card is Stalled, its controller chooses.
- **Made equipment** uses the Generic frame on the back of its Recipe; any class can equip it.
- **Life Seed:** a generic item (2 Aura). Sacrifice two Life Seeds to revive a dead Hero in your party with 5 Health Points; it's exhausted. A surrendered soul or a Hero Construct can't be revived.
- **Material Bender:** the first time each turn this Hero plays an item or activates an item's ability, that item's ability resolves twice (a "When this enters" ability when it plays the item, or the activated ability, like "Exile: Gain 1 Aura"). Its costs are paid once and its targets stay the same. Items other Heroes play or activate aren't doubled.
- **Gadget Commander:** its Strength equals the number of Crafted items (in your inventory or equipped) and Constructs (summons, including Dragon Constructs, and Hero Constructs) you control.
- **Artificer Heroes** (renamed Nov 15, 2026): Wren Partsfinder (was Artificer A), Material Bender (was Artificer B), Val Craftsmith (was Artificer C), Gadget Commander (was Artificer D), Cy the Builder (was Artificer E).
- **Deprecated Artificer cards** (Nov 14, 2026; not legal in decks): Clockwork Pair, Iron Guardians, Reclamation Kit Recipe, Blast Charge Recipe, Gearwork Hammer Recipe, Silver Edge Recipe, Mithril Blade Recipe, Warded Cloak Recipe.

## Glossary

Every term new in Set Two, alphabetical.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Abomination | A 5/5 summon token made by From Darkness. It enters with Stealth and ready. It does not have Impervious. |
| Construct | A summon made by an Artificer Recipe. |
| Craft | See Artificer. |
| Crafted | A subtype on items and equipment made by Recipes. See Artificer. |
| Descale | Whenever damage is dealt to this Hero, it permanently loses 1 Defense. Triggers even if Defense prevents all the damage. |
| Exhume | If this card was moved from your library to your discard pile this turn, you may play it from your discard pile. You still pay its costs. |
| Hero Construct | A Construct that joins your party as an extra Hero and acts Last. See Artificer. |
| Omni | A Hero type that counts as every Hero type (Remey the Incredible). See Artificer. |
| Impervious | This can't be dealt damage or destroyed. It still dies if it loses all its Health Points. Sacrificing it is allowed. |
| Material | Item subtype. In Set 2: the Ingots, Pearls and Dragon Scale. See Artificer. |
| Metal | "Any Metal" means any Ingot. See Artificer. |
| Tool | Equipment subtype for Artificer gear. See Artificer. |
| Mill X | Move the top X cards of a library to its discard pile. |
| Mourn | If you've surrendered a soul this game, you may play this card without paying its Aura cost. You still pay all other costs. Mourn is printed first on the card. |
| Quicksilver | See Artificer. |
| Recipe | See Artificer. |
| Remains | An item token made by Cursed Urn. Sacrifice it to gain 1 Aura, spent only on Summon cards. |
| Revenant | A 1/1 summon token with Impervious, made by Necromancer cards. Summoning one always exhausts the Hero. |
| Stall | See Artificer. |
| Stall counter | A counter put on a card to delay it. One is removed each time a player in the card owner's party takes an action. See Artificer. |
