# Set Three Rules Supplement
*Every rule and term that exists only in Set Three. It adds to the full Riverstead Rules; nothing here is defined in the main rules, so players without Set Three access never see it.*

*Playtest · updated Nov 27, 2026*

## About This Supplement

- **Who sees it:** only accounts with Set Three access on playriverstead.com (admins for now).
- **How it combines:** use the main rules for everything not covered here. If this supplement and the main rules disagree, this supplement wins in games that include Set Three cards.
- **Status:** playtest only. Every rule and card here can change between updates.
- **Lead class:** Death Knight. The Gambler is in design and isn't in this supplement yet.

## Death Knight

Death Knights are Paladins who died and were raised by evil power. They fight like Paladins, with Swords and Lances, but they have no soul. They're hard to put down, and the souls of the Heroes they kill are gone for good.

- **Heroes:** most Death Knights are 1 Strength, 1 Defense, 1 Aura, 17 Health Points. That's about 2 points more than a Paladin Hero, to make up for having no soul.
- **Eliazar the Fallen** is the exception: the fallen Paladin who kept his soul. He doesn't have Blight, and his soul works like any Hero's.

## Blight

**Blight** *(This hero has no soul. A hero it kills loses its soul. Neither can be revived or surrendered.)*

1. **No soul.** A Hero with Blight has no soul. When it dies, it can't be revived (by the Church or by a card) and its soul can't be surrendered. Leave it face down and turned sideways, like a Hero whose soul was surrendered.
2. **Blight kills.** A Hero killed by Blight loses its soul the same way: it can't be revived and its soul can't be surrendered.
3. **Blight is on the object.** A Hero with Blight gives its attacks Blight. A weapon or spell with Blight has Blight on its own, whoever uses it.
4. **Only the killing blow counts.** A death is a Blight kill only if the damage that killed the Hero came from something with Blight. Earlier hits don't add up: if a Blight attack wounds a Hero and a different card finishes it off, it isn't a Blight kill.
5. **Spells don't borrow Blight.** A Death Knight that casts a spell without Blight doesn't make a Blight kill, even if the spell kills.
6. **Health Point loss** (Poison, Warlock costs, Pain Pillars) is never a Blight kill unless the card causing it has Blight.
7. **Mourn and other "surrendered a soul" cards** only count souls that were actually surrendered. A soul lost to Blight was never surrendered.

## Rulings

- **Soul Harvester** levels up when any Hero dies, in either party. Leveling this way doesn't exhaust it and doesn't draw a card; it still gains a stat point at Levels 2, 4 and 6. Its Level 6 ability counts kills of enemy Heroes and summons.
- **Eliazar the Fallen at Level 6:** his attack hits its target, and the same attack also deals its damage to every other enemy Hero. Each hit checks Defense on its own.
- **Eliazar and Paladin equipment:** from Level 3 he can equip Paladin equipment. A party with Eliazar can put Paladin equipment in its deck.
- **Morwen Duskbane:** Poison, Burn, Wound, Confuse and Stun can't be put on her. Fatigue isn't a status counter.
- **Hollow Blade:** a Sword is any equipment with the Sword subtype (Knight's Sword, Long Sword, Sword of Riverstead, Sword of Eliazar).
